The source guide for *Zeitgeist: The Movie* makes an acknowledgment that changes how one of its comparisons should be read. The Horus story presented alongside the life of Jesus was assembled from motifs in different texts. The guide does not claim that the whole account appears together in one ancient source. It defends reconstruction as a way to recover the myth.

The comparison might recover an earlier story that Christians borrowed. It might instead create an apparent match because the later story guided which earlier details were selected.

The difficulty recurs across Peter Joseph's early *Zeitgeist* films. A religious parallel, an observation about a collapsing building and an account of money creation are each asked to contribute to a larger explanation of power. An underlying fact can be correct while the proposed connection to the next subject fails.

## Three subjects arranged as one argument

The first three films were *Zeitgeist: The Movie* in 2007, *Addendum* in 2008 and *Moving Forward* in 2011. They do not make an identical argument. The first moves from religion to September 11 and then central banking and political power. The later films develop an economic diagnosis and propose social redesign, prominently including Jacque Fresco's Venus Project.

Joseph's own account says the original began as a multimedia performance using music, instruments and video, then reached an unexpected audience after being placed on Google Video. That origin helps explain why the work combines an argument with a performance. The maker's reported audience totals are claims about circulation, not independent measurements of how many people accepted its conclusions.

The original's sequence connects the subjects before their histories have established the connection. Religious belief is treated as something assembled and used; the attack narrative becomes another account of public belief; finance supplies a continuing means of power. Read together, the sections suggest that apparently separate institutions help sustain an established order.

There are legitimate questions in that suggestion. Institutions can reinforce one another without sharing a secret director. A funding arrangement can favor one account over another. A government can exploit a crisis it did not create. Those possibilities call for different evidence from a claim that the same people manufactured the religion, attack and financial system. Describing all of them as control would leave the causal question unresolved.

Placing the arguments together can make them seem to corroborate one another. A viewer persuaded by a criticism of banking might feel that the religious section has gained support too. But a banking argument cannot date an Egyptian text. Nor can a disputed religious origin identify the cause of a building's collapse. The transitions have helped form a conclusion that still needs its historical support.

## A reconstructed Horus

The [companion guide](https://zeitgeistmovie.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/12/ZeitgeistThe-MovieCompanionGuidePDF.pdf#page=22) lays out the claims and their supporting references. It also contains later material, so it should not be treated as a word-for-word record of every version of the 2007 release.

Its explanation of Horus's twelve companions moves among solar figures, personified hours and groups associated with the sun god's journey. The guide uses those associations to support the familiar claim about twelve disciples. The category has widened in the process. A companion or a group in a cosmological scene does not automatically perform the role of a disciple in a biographical narrative.

That does not make the Egyptian material uninteresting. It changes the comparison being made. A study of recurring numbers and solar imagery could find important relationships without establishing that a particular Gospel episode was copied from a particular earlier account. To reach the latter conclusion, the argument needs to identify the relevant texts, their dates, the meaning of the figures in context and a plausible connection between traditions.

A comparison also needs some account of what would make the match less likely. Twelve can organize a calendar or a group without referring to a shared biography. A method that counts every occurrence as confirmation but never asks how common the number was elsewhere has given itself too little chance to be wrong. A more discriminating comparison would keep the narrative role and sequence in view, including differences that make borrowing harder to establish.

The reconstruction also needs to expose its choices. If material attached to several figures over a long period is combined under one name, the reader needs to know what has been combined. If broad terms such as helper or savior are used for different roles, the differences should remain visible. Otherwise a compact list can present an apparent match that a fuller account would qualify.

These problems can be identified without declaring that every ancient parallel is fabricated. The guide's own defense shows that the claimed earlier biography is partly the interpreter's construction. A reader can accept that religions borrow, adapt and reinterpret while declining this particular reconstruction. The detailed historical questions continue in [[astrotheology]]; they are not settled by rejecting religious authority in general.

## What free fall can establish

The September 11 section requires a different kind of investigation. The question is no longer how a motif traveled between texts, but what physical sequence caused a particular structure to fail. The guide gathers demolition claims, including arguments based on rapid descent. World Trade Center 7 provides a revealing case because the later official investigation explicitly addressed free fall.

NIST's [WTC 7 FAQ](https://www.nist.gov/world-trade-center-investigation/study-faqs/wtc-7-investigation) describes three stages in a measured portion of the north roofline's descent. Within the 5.4-second interval, the middle stage lasted 2.25 seconds and had gravitational acceleration. The agency interpreted it as descent with negligible support below, within its proposed sequence of prior structural failures. The surrounding stages had lower acceleration.

It would therefore be wrong to answer a free-fall argument by saying that NIST denied any free fall. It would also be wrong to treat the middle interval alone as a complete demonstration of explosives. It measures how that portion moved after support was lost. Identifying how the loss developed requires additional evidence and a model of the structure.

NIST concluded that fire-induced failures explained the collapse and that there was no evidence of a blast event. Its account can be examined through its assumptions, calculations and comparison with observations. An alternative would need to explain the physical sequence and account for the observations it claims the model misses.

Chronology matters here. The original film appeared in 2007, before NIST's final WTC 7 findings in 2008. A later guide can engage later material; an early film cannot be described as having answered a report not yet issued. The record should be compared across versions and dates, rather than treated as a single exchange frozen at release.

An observation that creates a difficulty for an explanation is valuable. It does not automatically identify a culprit, a device or a coordinated operation. Those are further claims. The wider history belongs in [[nine-eleven]], where separate questions can receive the space a film sequence cannot give them.

## Money creation without a hidden arithmetic

Commercial banks create deposit money when they lend. A bank is doing more than passing a saver’s existing deposit to a borrower. The Bank of England's 2014 paper [“Money creation in the modern economy”](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/2014/q1/money-creation-in-the-modern-economy) explains it directly and rejects the simple mechanical reserve-multiplier account as a description of actual practice.

A new loan gives the borrower a spendable deposit and a repayment obligation. The bank records a loan asset and a deposit liability. Both sides matter. The process creates money without giving everyone involved free wealth, and lending remains constrained.

For a simplified example, suppose a bank grants a £100 loan and credits £100 to the borrower's account. The borrower has a spendable balance and an equal obligation. If £20 of principal is then repaid from that account, with no other transactions, the remaining deposit and loan balance are £80 each. These hypothetical figures illustrate the entries. The example shows why the new deposit cannot be counted as a gift while the new debt is ignored.

None of that decides whether credit is allocated fairly, whether particular institutions receive political favors or how the burdens of a crisis are distributed. It does identify the mechanism those criticisms must address. Rejecting a misleading explanation of the mechanism should not become a defense of every outcome produced through it.

Central-bank governance is another question. The Federal Reserve's own description distinguishes a federal Board of Governors from twelve separately incorporated Reserve Banks and the stock that member banks must hold. Governors are nominated by the president and confirmed by the Senate; the Board is accountable to Congress. Reserve Bank stock differs from ordinary corporate ownership. This mixed structure deserves a more exact account than the claim that a private company simply owns the nation's money.

The institution's description is a starting point for examining its design, not an independent judgment that its decisions serve everyone equally. The questions of appointment, voting, discretion and benefit remain available for criticism. The criticism can then be directed at the body with the power to make the decision. [[federal-reserve]] and [[money-as-debt]] examine these issues further.

## Testimony and a proposed exit

The first film's source guide reproduces Aaron Russo's account of private conversations with Nicholas Rockefeller. A photograph and Russo's interview are offered in support of their relationship. Even if a relationship is established, it does not independently establish the reported conversation or the population-control intentions attributed to it. The claim of a coordinated [[new-world-order]] needs that additional evidence.

*Addendum* gives John Perkins a place in its account of economic power and presents the Venus Project as a possible alternative. Perkins's testimony can direct attention to particular projects, debts and decisions. It is still testimony. A general account of what an insider says an organization does needs to be tested against the records of the cases it purports to explain. A person’s experience can be genuine while a generalization from it is mistaken. The disputed historical claims are developed in [[economic-hitman]].

The move from diagnosis to Fresco's resource-based economy introduces another argument. A moneyless system would need ways to identify needs, coordinate production and resolve competing claims. Showing a failure in an existing arrangement does not show that a proposed replacement performs those tasks. It establishes a reason to look for alternatives, with the alternative still needing examination.

Consider a planned settlement that promises housing, transport and access to shared goods. A resident who disagrees with the chosen location or wants an unplanned service presents a practical problem for its administration. The administration would need to say what that person could change and who would decide which requests received resources first. A drawing can make an arrangement imaginable while leaving these decision rights unspecified. A trial of a shared transport service could measure journeys provided, materials used and requests left unmet. Its success would give the next experiment a basis, while leaving the argument for abolishing money to be made. The transition needs evidence between the drawing and the proposed worldwide system.

*Moving Forward* develops the structural economic argument further. The distributor and official descriptions emphasize monetary-market economics, behavior and redesign. An environment can shape incentives and opportunities without making every disagreement disappear. A proposed system should therefore explain how it handles conflict that remains, rather than rely on the assumption that suitable design removes the need for such an account. [[venus-project]] takes up that question.

## A movement is not every claim in a film

The films helped inspire the Zeitgeist Movement, but the filmmaker's site explicitly distinguishes the economic and sustainability movement from the detailed content of his films. The movement's FAQ, in a section dated 2025, describes dispersed activity without centralized coordination through its global website. That is its own account of its status, not a census of adherents or a measure of the films' remaining influence.

An interest in a resource-based economy does not commit someone to the first film’s religious or September 11 claims. The reverse also holds: changing the emphasis to social design leaves those earlier claims as well or poorly supported as they were before.

The film website now also announces *Zeitgeist: Requiem* for an official release in 2026. The project has continued beyond the three films examined here.

The first three films give a compact way to move from dissatisfaction with institutions to a proposal for replacing their foundations. Their force comes partly from the relation between the parts. The source guide makes it possible to interrupt that movement and inspect an individual step: a reconstructed myth, a measured interval of descent, an account of how a deposit is created.

Finding out how banks create a deposit need not settle what happened on September 11. A serious criticism of power should allow the discovery to remain valuable while the adjoining claim is challenged or abandoned.
