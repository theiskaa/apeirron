In its 27 August 2023 public account of the Lahaina fire, Hawaiian Electric emphasized that its lines were no longer energized when the destructive afternoon blaze appeared. The company acknowledged that damaged power lines appeared to have started a smaller fire that morning. It described the afternoon event as a second fire whose cause had not been determined.

The company’s account left two fires to explain. A year later, the Maui Fire Department's origin investigation, assisted by the federal Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives, concluded that there had been one fire in two phases. Embers had remained after the visible signs of burning disappeared.

The directed-energy theory offers a different explanation: Lahaina, Paradise and other communities were deliberately burned with lasers or related weapons. Photographs of surviving objects supply its most memorable evidence; military research supplies a possible technology. The fire investigations contain a more demanding record: damaged equipment, ignition times and the route by which a morning fire returned.

## The morning fire and the afternoon return

Maui's [October 2024 account of the investigation](https://www.mauicounty.gov/m/newsflash/Archive/Item/15840?arcId=18124) places the initial ignition at 6:34 a.m. on 8 August 2023, near utility pole 25 off Lahainaluna Road. Re-energized broken lines ejected hot metallic material into unmaintained vegetation. The morning fire was contained before 9 a.m.; for several hours, observers saw no flames, smoke or perceptibly burning material.

The investigators concluded that undetected embers remained and rekindled during a severe wind event at about 2:52 p.m. The finding connected the afternoon fire to the morning ignition. Hawaiian Electric's [October 2024 response](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/documents/about_us/news/2024/20241002_hawaiian_electric_statement_atf_report.pdf) acknowledged that conclusion, while emphasizing its understanding of the response timeline.

An electrical ignition does not require electricity to keep flowing throughout the subsequent fire. Once material is burning, shutting off the line removes one possible source of further ignition. Burning and smoldering fuel can continue producing heat without it. The [company's August 2023 statement](https://www.hawaiianelectric.com/documents/about_us/news/2023/20230827_lahaina_fires_update.pdf) about power being off in the afternoon therefore does not, by itself, establish an independent cause.

The utility’s early account and the later investigation agreed that a fire had burned in the area that morning. They differed over whether it had ever been fully extinguished. Evidence for an unrelated afternoon ignition would have to distinguish a new start from the rekindling investigators identified.

The investigation used witness interviews, photographs, video and electrical examination. Its public account also names an access limit: the utility supplied electrical data to ATF under confidentiality arrangements, and the Maui Fire Department was not given access to those data. The department relied on ATF's findings for that part of the analysis. The public finding thus combines shared evidence with an electrical-data analysis that only one of the two agencies could examine directly.

The fire was classified as accidental. That describes the origin investigators' conclusion about the ignition. Questions about preventability, maintenance, land management and the emergency response remain separate matters for evidence and accountability.

## Fire does not expose every object equally

The blue-object argument begins with a striking contrast: something blue survives while nearby buildings are destroyed. The proposed explanation is that a weapon selectively spared blue surfaces. But a photograph of a survivor does not tell us how its exposure compared with that of the objects that burned.

Wildfire spreads through several processes. Wind carries burning embers ahead of the main flames. An ember can enter a vulnerable part of a building or ignite material beside it. Nearby burning structures then produce more embers and expose other buildings to flames and radiant heat. The [National Institute of Standards and Technology's explanation](https://www.nist.gov/el/fire/resources/hazard-mitigation-methodology-hmm/why-implement-hmm-your-community/exposures) distinguishes these exposures and emphasizes that their intensity and duration vary.

Two adjacent buildings can therefore have different ignition histories. One may receive an ember at a vulnerable opening while the other does not. Separation, nearby fuels and barriers change the heat reaching a surface. The result can look selective without requiring anyone to have selected a target.

The Lahaina incident analysis documents a survivor: a house whose neighbors on all four sides were destroyed. One heat-exposed side had blistered paint and cracked windows but no signs of ignition. The analysts discussed separation from some neighboring structures, maintained vegetation and a concrete wall as factors that appeared to help shield the property. The damage records exposure to heat without ignition. [The report's built-environment cases](https://d1gi3fvbl0xj2a.cloudfront.net/2024-10/FSRI_Lahaina_Report__Redactions_10022024_Rev1%20%281%29.pdf) examine such differences rather than treating the final aerial image as a uniform exposure test.

A surface looks blue because of how it reflects visible light. Its response to radiation depends on the material and the wavelength involved, as [NASA's explanation of reflection and absorption](https://science.nasa.gov/ems/03_behaviors/) describes. Visible color does not establish immunity to every form of heating, nor can it identify the wavelength or source of an alleged weapon.

Testing a claim of color-based protection would require comparing objects with known materials and comparable exposures, recording both burned and surviving objects in each color category. Selecting photographs of blue survivors provides neither denominator. It cannot establish that color caused their survival.

The same difficulty applies to trees left standing among destroyed buildings. A surviving outline does not describe every part of an object's exposure or damage. A tree and a furnished house are different fuels with different geometry and vulnerabilities. Their different appearance after a fire is a question for fire analysis, rather than a demonstration that heat must have arrived as a beam.

## A weapon category is not an incident record

Directed-energy weapons are real. A [Congressional Research Service overview](https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/IF/PDF/IF11882/IF11882.10.pdf) describes high-energy lasers and high-powered microwave systems, including work intended to damage equipment or disrupt electronics. Concentrated electromagnetic energy can interact with and damage materials.

The technologies grouped under that label do different things. A laser concentrates light; microwave systems use a different part of the electromagnetic spectrum and can be designed to affect electronics. A claim of equipment disruption is not automatically an account of vegetation igniting. The proposed effect needs to fit the proposed device.

A [2023 Government Accountability Office overview](https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-23-106717) also describes limits involving distance, atmospheric conditions and cooling. Fog and storms can reduce the range and quality of a laser beam. Those constraints require an account of the actual operating conditions; a demonstration against one target does not certify performance against every other target at every distance.

For a weapon to explain Lahaina, it would have to enter the fire’s chronology. Where did its energy arrive, what ignited there, and how did that ignition produce the observed spread? A military demonstration elsewhere supplies none of those observations.

A bright streak in an image cannot complete that alternative on its own. Its date, place and original context have to be established before it can be placed in the fire's timeline. Even an authentic local image would need an explanation of what produced the light and how it connects to an ignition. The appearance of a line is not a record of a weapon firing.

Nor would a gap in the public military record prove the allegation. Classified work limits what an outside observer can know about capabilities. It does not establish which capability was present at a particular place or supply observations of its use.

## Paradise has a physical and a legal record

The 2018 Camp Fire in California offers a useful comparison because its cause and the utility's responsibility are extensively documented. The [CAL FIRE incident record](https://www.fire.ca.gov/incidents/2018/11/8/camp-fire) lists power lines as the cause and 85 civilian fatalities. The fire destroyed more than 18,000 structures, including much of Paradise.

A [2022 root-cause compilation](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/safety-policy-division/reports/root-cause-analyses-of-the-2017-18-wildfires.pdf), prepared for the California Public Utilities Commission and PG&E, reproduces CAL FIRE’s account of a supporting-hook failure on a transmission tower. The energized conductor contacted the tower, producing an electrical fault and hot material that fell into vegetation. The compilation also reproduces utility relay records showing the circuit opening around 6:15 a.m. on the day of the fire. Those records can be placed alongside the physical equipment findings and the investigator’s account of ignition. They are different forms of evidence with a common event to explain. A picture of the damage in Paradise cannot, on its own, recover that earlier sequence.

In June 2020, PG&E pleaded guilty to 84 counts of involuntary manslaughter and one count of unlawfully causing a fire. Its [filed financial report](https://www.cpuc.ca.gov/-/media/cpuc-website/divisions/energy-division/documents/electric-costs/ferc-form-1s/pge-2020q4-fercform14302021.pdf) records the pleas and sentencing. The 84 manslaughter counts and CAL FIRE's 85-fatality incident total are different records; one should not silently replace the other.

Criminal responsibility here was attached to an equipment failure and the company operating the line. It does not establish a second, concealed ignition mechanism. Replacing the hook, conductor and fault with a laser requires evidence that explains why that physical account is wrong and how the alternative fits the scene.

The distinction also prevents an easy mistake about Lahaina. Calling an ignition accidental does not determine whether the surrounding decisions were acceptable. The Camp Fire record shows how an electrical ignition and serious accountability can belong to the same account.

## The failures after ignition

The question of how a fire started is narrower than the question of why it became a disaster. Lahaina's response investigation makes that difference unusually concrete.

UL Research Institutes' Fire Safety Research Institute separately analyzed preparedness, infrastructure and response for Hawaiʻi's attorney general. Its [incident analysis](https://fsri.org/research-update/lahaina-fire-incident-analysis-report-released-attorney-general-hawaii) did not determine the fire's origin and cause.

Among its findings, the water analysis records that the two systems retained electricity at their production facilities and produced water at capacity during the fire. The storage-monitoring system failed at 3:30 p.m., leaving officials without tank-level data. Meanwhile, damaged household plumbing allowed water to escape from burned structures, reducing pressure and flow available in the fire area. [The findings and recommendations](https://ag.hawaii.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/FSRI_Lahaina_Report_Findings_and_Recommendations_09102024.pdf) describe a system overwhelmed by demands it could not meet.

The production facilities, monitoring connection and distribution pipes are separate parts of the system. Losing the monitor did not mean losing production. Producing water does not ensure that enough reaches a hydrant at usable pressure when damaged pipes are draining the network. Each damaged connection can become another outlet competing with firefighting demand. Across a burning neighborhood, many such outlets can be open at once. Reports of continued production and inadequate pressure can therefore both be true. Restoring a failed monitor would tell operators how much water remained. It would not close the damaged connections draining the tanks.

The report also says water personnel could not reach the fire area because of traffic and danger, while firefighters lacked the time, training or tools to shut off water. Its recommendation for better coordination addresses a practical obstacle: a leak that can be identified may still be difficult to isolate during an active fire.

## A later benefit does not identify an earlier cause

Fear about land and rebuilding after Lahaina was a serious public issue. In August 2023, Hawaiʻi's attorney general [asked property owners to report unsolicited purchase offers](https://governor.hawaii.gov/newsroom/attorney-general-lopez-urges-maui-property-owners-in-zip-codes-96761-96767-96790-to-report-unsolicited-purchase-offers/), referring to emergency restrictions covering specified areas. That intervention gave owners somewhere to report the approaches. It did not connect a purchaser to the ignition.

A purchaser can attempt to benefit from a catastrophe without having caused it. Establishing a deliberate burn-to-redevelop operation would require evidence connecting the proposed beneficiaries to the ignition or its preparation. A land offer after the fire cannot supply that connection by chronology alone.

The offer itself may still warrant scrutiny. Residents should not have to prove an energy-weapon plot for predatory conduct or failures of public administration to matter. Those issues can be pursued on the records of the transactions and decisions involved.

The initial Lahaina puzzle has a physical answer. An electrical supply can be shut down while a fire it started continues. The morning material, rather than an energized line in the afternoon, was the connection the investigators identified.
