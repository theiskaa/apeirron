On 3 June 2020, the World Economic Forum announced the Great Reset with its founder, Klaus Schwab, and Charles, then Prince of Wales. The pandemic was still unfolding. The proposal was to use the recovery to change how economies worked, rather than spend public money restoring everything as it had been.

The [launch announcement](https://www.weforum.org/media/the-great-reset-a-unique-twin-summit-to-begin-2021) proposed a new social contract and made the Reset the theme of a planned 2021 summit. Schwab and Thierry Malleret followed with a book, [announced that July](https://www.weforum.org/media/klaus-schwab-and-thierry-malleret-release-covid-19-the-great-reset-the-first-policy-book-on-the-covid-crisis-globally). A much larger allegation treats the initiative as part of an operation involving engineered crisis, compulsory digital identities, abolition of private ownership and government by an unelected international elite.

The documents make clear what their authors wanted. A later government decision has its own authors, procedures and source of authority. The connection between the two has to be established; matching the words in speeches does not establish who made the decision.

## Three proposals for the recovery

In his [launch-day article](https://www.weforum.org/stories/sustainable-development/now-is-the-time-for-a-great-reset), Schwab identified three priorities. Governments should make markets fairer through measures involving taxation, regulation, trade and competition. Recovery investment should support goals such as environmental sustainability and equality. New technologies should serve public needs, with pandemic diagnostics, treatments and other health applications offered as examples.

The article treated emergency disruption as evidence that rapid changes were possible and called for transformation across countries and industries. It also expected governments to act and businesses to participate throughout.

The breadth of the goals leaves consequential choices open. Equality does not specify a tax system. Sustainability does not decide who pays for a transition. A technological improvement for a hospital does not authorize every collection of personal data. Agreement with a goal cannot be treated as agreement with whichever measure a partner later proposes in its name.

Kristalina Georgieva's [remarks at the June launch](https://www.imf.org/en/news/articles/2020/06/03/sp060320-remarks-to-world-economic-forum-the-great-reset) gave the IMF's version of the recovery priorities: greener investment, a smaller digital divide and stronger social protection. These were substantive policy preferences, including investment incentives and carbon pricing. The launch brought these preferences into the same public conversation. Whether a particular loan, law or spending decision implemented one of them has to be traced through the institution responsible.

## Stakeholders still need a way to disagree

Stakeholder capitalism had already been set out before the pandemic. The [Davos Manifesto published in December 2019](https://www.weforum.org/stories/stakeholder-capitalism/davos-manifesto-2020-the-universal-purpose-of-a-company-in-the-fourth-industrial-revolution) described a company as serving employees, customers, suppliers, communities and society alongside its shareholders. It also said a multinational business should participate with governments and civil society in shaping a shared future.

This is the idea of stakeholder capitalism: corporate responsibilities extend beyond returns to owners. It can support an objection to a company that increases profits by passing costs to workers or neighbors. But naming those people as stakeholders does not settle how much authority they have over a decision.

A worker facing a plant closure and an investor seeking a higher return can both be recognized while remaining in conflict. The practical questions concern representation, access to information and the ability to refuse or challenge an outcome. If executives select the participants, set the agenda and decide what counts as success, the language of inclusion can leave the underlying balance of power intact.

The distinction becomes concrete when consultation ends in disagreement. Employees might be able to submit a view while the board retains the decision, or they might have a negotiated right that limits what the board can do. Both arrangements can be called stakeholder engagement. Only the second gives employees that particular constraint on the board’s power. Listening and negotiating a limit are different commitments.

This is also why corporate responsibility does not automatically grant corporate authority over public policy. A company can have obligations to a community without acquiring the right to speak for that community. Cooperation with business may be necessary for a project; representation of the people affected requires a further arrangement.

The Forum's [September 2020 reporting project](https://www.weforum.org/publications/measuring-stakeholder-capitalism-towards-common-metrics-and-consistent-reporting-of-sustainable-value-creation) provides a concrete expression of its doctrine. Developed with the major accounting firms, it proposed 21 core and 34 expanded metrics and disclosures. Its [tables](https://www.mgtc.gov.my/storage/2023/05/WEF-ESG-Disclosure-Guidance.pdf) include greenhouse-gas emissions and pay equality, drawing on existing reporting standards.

The pay-equality metric, for example, compares salary and remuneration across employee groups, including women and men, within categories and significant locations. It gives a reader something more specific than an assurance that the company values fairness. Understanding the result still requires the categories and coverage: an aggregate label cannot tell the reader which jobs have been compared.

The report encouraged companies to use metrics they considered material and appropriate, with an explanation where they did not disclose. This was a framework for company reporting. It did not establish a personal score for every citizen or a law requiring people to meet behavioral targets.

Reporting could make an otherwise hidden cost visible. It could also allow a company to advertise a commitment without giving affected people a remedy. Publishing a pay ratio makes a claim that can be checked. Closing the gap would require action on pay, and giving employees a stronger say would require a change in decision rights. A reporting commitment alone promises neither.

## What the UN partnership actually arranged

A [June 2019 framework between the United Nations and the Forum](https://static.mediapart.fr/files/2020/05/10/un-wef-strategicpartnership.pdf) shows a route for institutional cooperation before the Reset launch. It covered areas including finance, climate, health, digital cooperation and education. It proposed sharing networks, using communication platforms, arranging participation in meetings and reviewing the partnership through regular leadership and technical contact.

The text made cooperation subject to the institutions' respective mandates and legal frameworks. It said the arrangement was not intended to be legally binding and that each party would bear its own costs. Those provisions matter when the document is presented as a transfer of government to the Forum. The document describes access and collaboration; it does not grant the Forum a legislature or replace national institutions.

The concern about access is substantial on its own. In a [September 2019 open letter](https://www.tni.org/es/art%C3%ADculo/pon-fin-al-acuerdo-de-asociacion-estrategica-entre-las-naciones-unidas-onu-y-el-foro?translation=en), civil-society organizations called for the partnership's termination. They argued that it would give transnational corporations privileged access and weaken public accountability. The criticism preceded the pandemic and concerned an identifiable agreement.

Calling the framework nonbinding does not answer that criticism. Regular consultation can influence which issues are discussed first, which options are developed and whose practical knowledge is treated as indispensable. The agreement is evidence of an organized relationship. Demonstrating that the relationship determined a particular policy would require the policy's record as well.

A meaningful review could ask who attended the relevant working meetings, what alternatives were considered and whether affected communities had comparable opportunities to shape the result. A meeting record could establish who helped frame an issue. A draft and its revisions could show which proposal survived. A contract could establish obligations that a press release did not. Each would add a different part of the account; simply knowing that two organizations were partners would leave those parts missing. A claim that every later decision came from one command center passes over these intermediate decisions.

## A recovery fund has its own decision record

The European recovery program gives this distinction a public record to follow: green and digital investment appear both in the Reset language and in actual public spending rules.

The [European Commission proposed NextGenerationEU on 27 May 2020](https://ec.europa.eu/commission/presscorner/api/files/document/print/en/ip_20_940/IP_20_940_EN.pdf), a week before the Reset launch. Its proposal described a recovery instrument of €750 billion and emphasized environmental and digital investment. Schwab's June article referred to the European proposal while arguing for the direction recovery money should take.

In [February 2021](https://www.consilium.europa.eu/en/press/press-releases/2021/02/11/eu-recovery-package-council-adopts-recovery-and-resilience-facility/), the Council adopted the regulation establishing the Recovery and Resilience Facility. Its account specified that at least 37 percent of each national plan's allocation should support the green transition and at least 20 percent digital transformation. National governments would submit plans, the Commission would assess them and the Council would make approval decisions. Later payments depended on agreed milestones and targets.

The spending conditions came through an EU regulation and a specified approval process. An objection to them can be directed at the institutions that established and applied them. The earlier Commission proposal also makes a simple story of June's announcement creating the policy untenable. Earlier influence would be a separate historical claim to investigate. Evidence might include an acknowledged source for a proposal, documented participation in drafting or records showing that officials adopted a specific recommendation. A shared environmental objective alone cannot distinguish that route from policies developed through other relationships.

If a national plan funds a project that residents oppose, its approval, procurement and consultation records provide places to look. Attributing the decision only to Davos can leave the officials and contractors who exercised actual powers strangely out of focus.

## A future without ownership, written in 2016

Several years before the Reset launch, Danish politician Ida Auken’s [2016 scenario, republished by the Forum](https://medium.com/world-economic-forum/welcome-to-2030-i-own-nothing-have-no-privacy-and-life-has-never-been-better-ee2eed62f710), imagined a person living in 2030 without owning a home, car, clothes or appliances. Goods had become services; the narrator could have kitchen equipment delivered instead of storing it.

Many services in the story had become free, and the city-dweller paid no rent because unused space was shared. It also described a loss of privacy and people who had been excluded from, or rejected, the city. These features belong to the same scenario. Omitting either its promised abundance or its costs changes the argument it presents.

A scenario lets a writer explore consequences by imagining conditions that do not yet exist. It does not show how a government would legally abolish ownership, or establish that the publisher has adopted such a policy. This piece also preceded the June 2020 initiative by several years. Attaching it to the Reset without its date and form turns an imagined future into an alleged implementation schedule.

There are good questions inside the fiction. If access replaces ownership, who controls the service? Can a person leave without losing the things needed to live? What protects privacy, and what happens to people outside the system? The narrator's optimism does not answer them.

Publishing the scenario gave those assumptions a prominent platform. It is fair to criticize the future being imagined and the ease with which its exclusions are accommodated. The stronger claim—that it documents a compulsory worldwide confiscation program—needs an implementation record the story does not contain.

## Planning an outbreak and causing one

Event 201 is often attached to the Reset as evidence that the pandemic and its response had already been scripted. The [Johns Hopkins Center for Health Security's account](https://centerforhealthsecurity.org/our-work/tabletop-exercises/event-201-pandemic-tabletop-exercise) describes a different kind of event: a three-and-a-half-hour tabletop exercise held on 18 October 2019, with the Forum and Gates Foundation as partners. Participants discussed problems presented through a fictional outbreak.

The [prepared pathogen sheet](https://centerforhealthsecurity.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/caps-fact-sheet-191009.pdf) explicitly labeled its coronavirus fictional. Its proposed history involved transmission through pigs and spillover to people in South America. The exercise supplied assumptions so participants could discuss the consequences. Similarity to a later disease does not turn those assumptions into observations made in advance. In a tabletop exercise, participants can use information the organizers have written into the scenario; they are testing responses to those stipulated conditions. To claim foreknowledge of the real outbreak, one would need information about it that the fictional assumptions do not supply.

Its [published recommendations](https://centerforhealthsecurity.org/sites/default/files/2022-12/200117-publicprivatepandemiccalltoaction.pdf) can be examined without that leap. They advocated advance public–private arrangements, medical supply capacity and continuity of travel and trade. The latter is worth noticing when the exercise is described as a rehearsal for shutting borders: the recommendation sought to maintain key routes despite the pressure to stop movement.

The same document also called for cooperation with media companies, prioritizing authoritative health messages and suppressing messages judged false. That raises questions about standards, error correction and who can challenge a designation. Describing the exercise accurately does not require endorsing every recommendation.

Neither the training materials nor the recommendations establish that their authors caused COVID-19 or controlled each government's subsequent response. To show that a recommendation became a particular restriction, an investigation would need evidence of its adoption and the authority used. The content of a planning exercise is a starting document for that inquiry, not its conclusion.

## Where the power question becomes answerable

The Forum does cultivate relationships. Its [Young Global Leaders program](https://www.younggloballeaders.org/about) describes leadership development and an international network. Such a network can create access, introduce people to shared ideas and help them find collaborators. Membership alone cannot establish why an alumnus made a decision, and selecting people already considered promising complicates claims that the program created their later success.

The broad label “Great Reset” becomes less useful when it absorbs every digital identity system, banking proposal, environmental rule and health measure. Each has a design and an institution capable of adopting it. A data system's privacy protections, a central bank's legal authority and a corporate reporting framework's obligations cannot be inferred from their appearance at the same conference. The related [[cbdc]] and [[mass-surveillance]] questions need those specific records.

The democratic criticism remains: people with substantial economic resources can gain repeated access to officials and help define what counts as a practical solution. Publicity does not make that access representative. A published agenda can still be narrow about whose interests it accommodates.

The partnership’s access and the recovery facility’s spending rules deserve scrutiny through their own records. The point at which a private preference becomes public policy is where an institution with authority adopts it. That is where its reasons, obligations and means of challenge should be visible.
