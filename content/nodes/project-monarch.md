Project Monarch is an allegation about turning childhood abuse into a technology of control. In its familiar form, the story describes a secret program that deliberately creates separate identity states, assigns them tasks, and allows a handler to activate them while the person remains unaware. It is usually presented as a continuation of the CIA's MKUltra research.

The allegation combines things with very different kinds of support. The CIA conducted abusive experiments. People can experience serious disturbances of memory and identity. Sexual exploitation can be organized and sustained by people with money and influence. None of those facts, taken separately or together, demonstrates the particular programming system described in Monarch literature.

Monarch took shape where those histories met therapeutic accounts, memoirs, and interpretations of public events. Following that process explains both its appeal and the distance between its claims and the evidence offered for them.

## What the name is meant to explain

“Mind control” can describe several different claims. A threat can force someone to comply. Dependence on an abuser can make leaving dangerous. Drugs can alter perception, and interrogation can exhaust or disorient a person. These are already consequential ways of affecting behavior, with different mechanisms and limits.

Monarch's defining claim goes further. It proposes a durable system in which an operator can establish compartments of identity and memory, assign them separate functions, and retrieve those functions on demand. The person might allegedly serve as a courier, a sexual operative, or an assassin without knowing what another identity state had done. Some versions extend the system to entertainers and public figures.

Proponents describe programming categories and symbolic triggers as though they were parts of a technical manual. The detail makes the account feel internally organized. But a vocabulary can organize a story before anyone has shown that it describes a working process. A label for an alleged function is not a record of someone reliably producing it.

Evidence that a person was drugged establishes an event worth investigating. Evidence that the person later had trouble recalling it may establish an additional injury. To establish the Monarch account, we would also need to show that someone could deliberately store a specified task outside ordinary awareness and retrieve it reliably under the claimed conditions. That is a much larger achievement than producing distress or confusion.

A history of attempted mind control has to preserve that difference between the goal, the procedure, and the result. Otherwise every injury becomes a successful experiment simply because the researcher intended to change a person's mind.

## The record Monarch borrows from

The 1977 [Senate hearing on MKUltra](https://www.intelligence.senate.gov/sites/default/files/hearings/95mkultra.pdf) describes a government research program, surviving financial files, and an effort to reconstruct work after many records had been destroyed in 1973. It documents research into behavior and the use of unwitting subjects. This is an evidential foundation made of authorizations, accounts, correspondence, and testimony about institutions that could be identified and questioned.

The destruction created genuine uncertainty. It prevented a full accounting and denied affected people information that should have been available to them. The remaining files were not a complete catalogue of every intervention or everyone harmed.

Yet an incomplete record still has boundaries. If an investigator finds a grant supporting an experiment, the grant can establish funding without demonstrating that the experiment achieved its purpose. If a file is missing, its absence cannot tell us which of several proposed activities it described. The institution responsible for destruction deserves scrutiny; a later writer does not acquire knowledge of the contents by pointing to the missing box.

Ewen Cameron's research in Montreal supplies a specific connection to the ambitions Monarch describes. In March 1957, the CIA approved a grant for work identified as Subproject 68. Cameron combined methods he called depatterning with psychic driving, which involved repeated recorded messages. His research application sought ways of making the depatterning more effective.

The nine plaintiffs in [*Orlikow v. United States*](https://law.justia.com/cases/federal/district-courts/FSupp/682/77/1583126/) had been Cameron's patients during the grant period. Each had received at least one part of the procedure. The court recorded that they had not known of its alleged experimental nature. In 1988, the judge found that factual disputes prevented resolving many of the government's arguments at that stage; the opinion was not a trial judgment establishing every allegation. It nevertheless makes the institutional questions concrete: what the agency funded, what procedures patients received, and what they were told.

Monarch accounts often compress this history into personality erasure followed by successful reconstruction. That wording gives the second part a certainty the evidence does not supply. Memory loss, disorientation, and difficulty functioning are injuries; they do not show that a researcher could install a chosen replacement identity. A person harmed by an intervention is not thereby proof that its designer understood the mind.

The fuller institutional history belongs in [[mkultra]]. Its place here is as a documented precedent for covert experimentation and failed accountability. A proposed successor would need evidence of its own organization, participants, methods, and results. Shared ambition is a reason to investigate a connection, not the connection itself.

## A lecture gives the story clinical authority

A widely circulated transcript dates D. Corydon Hammond's lecture “Hypnosis in MPD: Ritual Abuse” to June 25, 1992, in Alexandria, Virginia. It later became known as the Greenbaum speech. Hammond described patients reporting an organized programming system and treated similarities between their accounts as evidence of coordination. The transcript's introduction lists his professional qualifications, giving readers a reason to take his clinical observations seriously.

His account also describes consulting therapists, suggesting subjects to investigate, and comparing the responses they obtained. He said he tried to prevent contamination, sometimes withholding background information. The [circulating transcript](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/52e9ece3e4b074062a41314d/t/5c53a2e1104c7bd8f7751256/1548985059033/The%2BGreenbaum%2BSpeech,%2BCorry%2BHammond.pdf) preserves his claims; it does not supply an independently reviewable set of clinical records proving a government program.

Suppose several clinicians report that patients have supplied similar descriptions. If the accounts were recorded before any of those people encountered the common vocabulary, the resemblance might be informative. If the clinicians shared a framework and then used it to ask questions, some agreement could have entered through the questions themselves.

Geographical separation would not resolve that problem. A therapist in another state could attend the same conference, read the same literature, or consult someone already familiar with the account. Patients could have encountered material independently too. Establishing independence means following the history of contact and information, not just locating addresses on a map.

A convincing comparison would preserve the early accounts, the exact questions, the sequence of consultations, and the cases that failed to match. It would also separate spontaneous descriptions from recognition after a possibility had been offered. Without that sequence, later similarity cannot tell us how much was present at the beginning.

Clinical training can help someone recognize distress and provide care. It does not let them identify the historical cause of a patient's experience merely from how convincing an account feels in the consulting room. Claims about a hidden organization require investigation outside that room.

## From testimony to a larger system

Books helped give Monarch a recognizable public form. Cathy O'Brien and Mark Phillips published *Trance Formation of America* in 1995. O'Brien presents her experience as participation in an intelligence-run program; the [authors' website](https://trance-formation.com/) continues to identify the account with MKUltra and Monarch. It is a primary source for what they claim, rather than an independent confirmation of those claims.

Fritz Springmeier and Cisco Wheeler's *The Illuminati Formula Used to Create an Undetectable Total Mind Controlled Slave*, published in 1996, offered an extensive account of an alleged system. In the literature surrounding these accounts, personal testimony, clinical terminology, intelligence history, and interpretations of symbolism reinforce one another.

This creates a problem when a later work cites an earlier one. A claim can accumulate references without accumulating witnesses. Three books may repeat one account; a website may cite those books and appear to add a fourth source. To assess the underlying evidence, a reader has to follow the references back until reaching a person, a document, or an observation that can be examined separately.

A memoir can contain an accurate recollection, an interpretation adopted later, a mistake, and a claim supported by outside records. Those parts need not all stand or fall together. Checking a journey or an address would support that detail. It would not automatically authenticate an allegation about what happened there or identify the organization responsible.

The same care applies in the other direction. A factual error does not establish that every experience described was invented. An unsupported claim about a government program does not tell us that the person suffered no abuse. Judging an entire life by a verdict on a theory is a poor substitute for examining the events being alleged.

The most revealing question for a purported insider account is what it allows someone else to check. A participant may know a location, identify a contemporaneous record, or describe a procedure whose result can be independently tested. When the response to every request is that the evidence is inaccessible by design, the account gives readers little means to distinguish knowledge from conviction.

## Memory is part of the evidence

The memory controversy surrounding Monarch is often presented as a choice between believing survivors and believing that therapy invents abuse. That choice leaves out the actual work of evaluating a recollection. People may remember events after a long interval; people may also acquire a sincere belief in something that did not happen as they now recall it.

The American Psychological Association's [archived discussion of childhood-abuse memories](https://www.apa.org/topics/trauma/memories) recognizes both later recollection and the possibility of suggestion. The American Psychiatric Association's [position on memory-focused therapies](https://www.psychiatry.org/getattachment/930fb215-2147-40e9-9d44-f06d84fc64de/Position-2013-Memories-Child-Abuse.pdf), reaffirmed in 2013, cautions against judging the historical accuracy of uncorroborated new memories from observations in psychotherapy. It also warns that dismissing true accounts and accepting false ones can both cause harm.

In the study Elizabeth Loftus describes in [“Creating False Memories”](https://pthspsychology.weebly.com/uploads/9/2/6/1/9261051/loftus_creating_false_memories.pdf), participants received accounts of childhood events presented with help from relatives. One event—being lost in a shopping mall—was invented. After repeated opportunities to recall the events, some participants reported memories of the invented episode; six of twenty-four continued to do so at follow-up.

That result shows that a sincere autobiographical report can arise from suggestion in a particular setting. It does not show that every delayed account of abuse is false, or that an ordinary laboratory procedure reproduces prolonged trauma. Confidence and detail alone cannot certify the origin of a memory.

For a historical inquiry, the circumstances of recall therefore matter. What did the person report before treatment? Which details appeared after questions, books, conversations, or group sessions? Are there records made near the alleged event? Does another witness supply information independently, or repeat what the first person has since told them?

A record dated before therapy can supply information that later suggestion cannot explain away. A separately documented event can anchor part of an account even while other parts remain uncertain.

Care and historical verification have different jobs. Someone's distress can deserve attention while the identity of a perpetrator or the existence of a program remains unresolved. Turning agreement with a theory into a condition of compassion serves neither purpose well.

## A diagnosis does not identify a handler

Dissociative identity disorder, formerly called multiple personality disorder, is a recognized clinical diagnosis. The American Psychiatric Association's [description](https://www.psychiatry.org/patients-families/dissociative-disorders/what-are-dissociative-disorders) includes distinct identity states, gaps in memory, and distress or impairment. Its account associates the disorder with overwhelming experiences and childhood trauma. These are clinical descriptions of difficulties a person experiences.

Monarch adds an external designer who allegedly engineers the states and controls their behavior. That designer is not part of the diagnosis. A clinician identifying dissociative symptoms has not thereby identified a perpetrator, an intelligence affiliation, or a technology for assigning tasks.

It is also misleading to describe people with the diagnosis as collections of independent agents housed in one body. Different identity states are experiences of one person. Language borrowed from computers—files, switches, codes, programs—can make an alleged mechanism easy to imagine while encouraging readers to overlook that person.

A diagnosis cannot be used to label a celebrity from a video clip either. A performer may act, change a stage persona, forget a line, or appear distressed. None gives a distant viewer the history and assessment needed to explain what they are seeing. Adding the Monarch vocabulary can turn a brief public image into a detailed private story that the image never contained.

Butterfly imagery gives that process a familiar starting point. A costume, a photograph, or a scene of transformation is read as a reference to Monarch, then the reference is treated as evidence of participation. Even if a designer deliberately used the theory's imagery, that would establish a creative choice. Establishing an abusive practice would require access to what happened beyond the image: the people involved, their conduct, and records of it. The visual interpretation cannot do that work on its own.

## What investigation of abuse can establish

Monarch developed alongside claims of organized ritual abuse that circulated during the 1980s and early 1990s. Some accounts joined allegations about cults, government experiments, and deliberately created identity states. A shared concern about child abuse brought clinicians, investigators, and campaigners into contact, while disagreements over evidence could become accusations of indifference or complicity.

FBI investigator Kenneth Lanning's 1992 [guide to allegations of ritual child abuse](https://ojp.gov/pdffiles1/Digitization/136592NCJRS.pdf) offered a more useful approach. He distinguished the perspectives of family members, therapists, social workers, and criminal investigators. He urged investigation and corroboration of particular crimes rather than acceptance of an entire explanatory system. He also allowed that an account might contain accurate events alongside distorted or contaminated elements.

His approach does not require investigators to solve every allegation before acting on one that has support. Evidence of a specific assault can matter even when a broader account includes claims investigators cannot substantiate. Conversely, evidence that an adult abused a child cannot establish every alleged ceremony, organization, or technical procedure attached to the case.

This is why later evidence of organized sexual exploitation has to be read carefully. Maxwell's December 2021 conviction concerned her role in facilitating and participating in abuse with Jeffrey Epstein, as the [prosecutor's statement records](https://www.justice.gov/usao-sdny/pr/statement-us-attorney-damian-williams-verdict-us-v-ghislaine-maxwell). It establishes a serious history of abuse. It does not demonstrate the production of controllable identity states.

The [[epstein]] case merits examination through its testimony, financial relationships, communications, and institutional decisions. Those forms of evidence can reveal recruitment, assistance, knowledge, and protection. Calling the whole case Monarch before tracing them risks making a speculative mechanism the center of attention while identifiable acts recede into the background.

## Historical links need people and records

The proposed Nazi origin supplies another route by which established crimes enter the Monarch account. Some versions identify Josef Mengele or a figure called Greenbaum with the development of the alleged system, then invoke [[operation-paperclip]] to explain a transfer to American intelligence.

Mengele conducted criminal experiments at Auschwitz. The United States Holocaust Memorial Museum's [account of his life](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/josef-mengele) describes his escape to South America and death in Brazil in 1979. The National Archives' [review of released CIA files](https://www.archives.gov/iwg/declassified-records/rg-263-cia-records/rg-263-report.html) describes material about his hiding places, reported sightings, associates, and the later investigation. The existence of an intelligence file on someone does not make that person an intelligence employee.

A transfer of a specific method would require a more precise history: who developed it, where it was recorded, who obtained the material, and what another organization subsequently did with it. Proof that American institutions employed some people with Nazi pasts cannot fill those steps for any named individual or any proposed technique.

That precision also prevents the victims of one documented crime from becoming scenery for another story. Mengele's crimes stand on their own historical evidence. They do not acquire an additional programming purpose because a later account needs an origin for its mechanism.

## What would change the account

The Monarch allegation could become better supported through a traceable record identifying an operation, contemporaneous evidence connecting participants to particular acts, or a credible demonstration of the specific capability being claimed. A program name on a list would need context. A record of research interest would need results. Independent accounts would need a history showing how their independence was established.

The records and accounts examined here do not provide that chain. They supply a documented history of abusive research and a later literature interpreting suffering, memory, and exploitation through a much more ambitious model of control. The first history explains why people might suspect the second. It does not make the second an established account.

There are still worthwhile investigations to pursue without assuming Monarch. Institutions can be asked what they funded, which records survive, and how they responded to people alleging harm. Specific allegations can be checked against dates, locations, witnesses, and documents. Those inquiries can identify responsibility without requiring anyone to accept a complete theory in advance.

The questions that can be answered are often smaller than the theory: who approved a grant, who received an intervention, who knew about an allegation, and who failed to act. Answering them can identify harm and responsibility even where the larger account remains unsupported.
