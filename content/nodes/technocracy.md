A transport engineer can estimate how long a bus takes to complete a route. Deciding whether that route should continue is a different task. A lightly used service might cost more per passenger than a busy one while providing the only practical journey to work for a few households. The timetable, subsidy and distribution of inconvenience belong to the same decision. Knowing how to calculate the cost does not settle who should bear it.

Technocracy begins where confidence in technical knowledge becomes a claim about who should govern. The word also names a specific American movement associated with Howard Scott during the Depression. Its members proposed replacing the money economy with a continent-wide system of production and distribution measured in energy. Those two meanings are related, but they need separate histories. A government employing economists has not thereby adopted Scott's plan.

The old proposal makes the attraction unusually clear. If machinery can produce abundance, why should financial arrangements prevent people from receiving it? The inventory still leaves people with reasons to disagree about what should be produced and who should receive it.

## The promise behind the accounts

Thorstein Veblen had attacked the relationship between business and industrial production before Scott's movement became prominent. In *The Engineers and the Price System*, published in 1921 from earlier essays, he used sabotage to describe restrictions on productive capacity that maintained profitable prices. The word included ordinary business practices, rather than only violent damage to machinery. A plant could be technically capable of making more goods while its owner had a financial reason to keep output down.

Veblen described a conflict: industrial efficiency and business success could pull in different directions. His later discussion of technicians explored reorganizing control around those who understood production. These were arguments about whose interests governed the factory, as well as about how efficiently it operated.

That diagnosis has force whenever unmet need coexists with unused capacity. Replacing money with a physical unit of account would address that conflict only if the new system could also handle scarcity and disagreement.

Scott's publicity during the Depression offered the physical answer. His association with a group working at Columbia helped give the movement academic standing. An Associated Press report printed in *The Daily Iowan* on 24 January 1933 recorded the withdrawal of Walter Rautenstrauch and three associates from their connection with Technocracy. It also reported that Columbia would continue its own energy and technology survey, employing engineers and architects who had worked on the earlier project. The episode was a rupture within an actual movement, not the disappearance of every argument for expert administration. Nor does a university association establish that its proposals had received a university's scientific endorsement.

The [*Technocracy Study Course*](https://archive.org/download/TechnocracyStudyCourse1945/TechnocracyStudyCourse-1945.pdf#page=243) set out the proposed administration. In its 1945 edition, the design assigns technical functions to an administrative hierarchy. A governing body called the Continental Control selects the Continental Director from its own members; ordinary appointments come from above. The director can be recalled by a two-thirds decision of that controlling body. The electorate has no corresponding place in this arrangement.

The consumption scheme sets a [two-year energy budget](https://archive.org/download/TechnocracyStudyCourse1945/TechnocracyStudyCourse-1945.pdf#page=249), deducts collective provision and distributes equal energy-denominated incomes to adults over twenty-five. Personal allotments cannot be transferred or saved into the next period. Identifiable certificates record purchases through a Distribution Sequence. These are proposed operating rules, not descriptions of an economy that was built.

The promise of equal access is part of the appeal. Income would cease to depend on an employer needing someone's labor. In return, the public would rely on a common apparatus to determine provision and recognize its claims. Whether that bargain offers security depends on more than the quantity initially allotted: it also depends on what happens when someone challenges the apparatus.

## What an energy account leaves undecided

An energy budget could identify a real limit. A plan requiring more fuel or generating capacity than is available cannot be carried out merely by issuing additional claims on it. Money does not make the physical requirement disappear.

An adequate energy supply would not settle how it should be used. Two goods requiring the same energy to produce need not have the same importance to someone who needs one of them urgently. A replacement part that keeps a water pump operating can matter more than another object with the same production requirement. Location, timing, alternative uses and the consequences of going without also enter the choice. Energy accounting describes one constraint; it does not supply a complete ranking of needs.

Consider the bus route again. Suppose two possible schedules consume the same fuel. One offers frequent service during a short commuting period; the other spreads fewer journeys across the day. The first may serve more workers. The second may suit someone attending a medical appointment or working an irregular shift. Better passenger counts could clarify the effects. They would still leave a decision about which forms of access deserve support.

Equal allotments can meet unequal needs poorly. A person with a disability may require services another person does not. A household's location can change the resources needed for an ordinary journey. Collective provision could address such differences, but then its size and design must be decided before the equal remainder is distributed. Moving an expense into the common budget does not remove the decision about it.

These objections do not establish that market prices always distribute goods fairly. Someone may need a service badly and lack the money to express that need as effective demand. Nor does a price automatically include every cost imposed on people outside the transaction. Public provision and regulation can have reasons that a private sale leaves out. The question for the energy scheme is whether its replacement account handles these difficulties, rather than merely giving them a different unit.

Saving introduces another claim on the future. An unspent certificate is a claim on provision; unused fuel is a physical resource. Cancelling the claim does not destroy the fuel. Conversely, carrying fuel forward does not give its former claimant control over it later. The proposed rules change who can make future decisions. Calling them bookkeeping would conceal that transfer of authority.

## The knowledge outside headquarters

Friedrich Hayek's 1945 essay, “The Use of Knowledge in Society,” offers a different reason to doubt comprehensive direction. Relevant knowledge is dispersed. Technical expertise matters, but so do changing circumstances known to particular people: an available machine, a delayed delivery, a use that has suddenly become urgent.

His example is a change in the scarcity of tin. A price change can encourage users to economize or substitute without their knowing the original cause. The information travels through their adjustments. Hayek's argument concerns how people coordinate while each knows only part of the situation; it does not depend on everyone possessing a complete explanation of the economy.

A modern planner might answer that computing and communications make much more information available than Hayek could have gathered. That is a serious reply to a particular data limitation. It still needs to distinguish recording a circumstance from learning what people will do when it changes. A database of current journeys cannot contain every journey someone would make under a different timetable, fare or place of work.

More information also leaves uncertainty about the future. For the bus service, a forecast might assume that fuel costs, employment locations and passenger habits remain within a certain range. Testing a shorter timetable for a limited period could reveal something a model misses. Publishing the assumptions would let residents point out a shift pattern the planners had overlooked. Technical advice becomes more useful when its uncertainty is available for other people to work with.

People also adapt to the measures used to judge them. If a hypothetical transport office is rewarded only for reducing cost per passenger, removing a difficult route could improve its result while leaving some residents worse off. The arithmetic may be correct. The choice of measure would be doing political work before anyone reached the final calculation.

Competence is also specific to a task. Hiring someone who understands electrical circuits is sensible. Giving that person authority to decide every claim on a community's electricity requires additional reasons. Reliability, affordability, industrial use and protection for vulnerable households may conflict. Expertise can explain the feasible combinations and their likely consequences. It cannot turn every conflict into a wiring error.

## Independence with a specified boundary

Modern delegation sometimes deliberately protects a decision maker from immediate political instructions. The European Central Bank describes its independence in terms of a treaty framework: neither it nor the national central banks may take instructions from governments or EU institutions. Its stated purpose is to carry out its mandate, centered on price stability.

The ECB also describes [accountability to the European Parliament](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/ecb/our-values/accountability/html/index.en.html), including annual reporting, quarterly hearings of its president before the relevant committee and published replies to written questions. Those arrangements deserve examination alongside independence. They provide ways to question and assess the institution; they do not let a parliamentary hearing issue an instruction on each monetary decision.

A defender can argue that stable rules protect decisions from short-term electoral pressure. A critic can ask whether the mandate is too narrow, whether its distributional effects receive adequate consideration, or whether explanation after a decision supplies enough control. The mandate and the reporting duties give that dispute something concrete to address. Describing the bank as simply elected or unelected leaves too much unexplained.

A bounded monetary mandate, operating in a system of money and markets, differs from replacing that system with an administrative allocation of consumption. One can object to the first without pretending it already contains every feature of the second.

## The proposed line to global institutions

Patrick Wood argues for a much stronger continuity. His public account connects the Trilateral Commission and sustainable development to a modern technocratic project. His 2015 book *Technocracy Rising* gives that interpretation an extended form. It is a claim about the direction and origins of institutions, not merely a loose use of the word expert.

Wood's account concerns a real network. The State Department's published documentary history identifies its establishment in 1973 and Brzezinski's role as director. It also records Carter's attendance at its 1975 meeting and Brzezinski's subsequent work on Carter's foreign-policy positions. Such connections help explain how people and ideas reached a future administration. They do not, by themselves, connect that network to Technocracy Incorporated's chain of command or its energy certificates. The relationship is developed in [[cfr-trilateral]].

A letter proposing that the Commission adopt the earlier design would establish a connection that shared resource vocabulary cannot. So would a recorded transfer of organizational authority. Without such a connection, the people involved could be pursuing a different project with some similar concerns.

The differences in the policies also matter. [[agenda-21]] recommends measures concerning land markets, tenure and local planning. Its text includes help for poorer people to obtain credit to buy land. The [[great-reset]] proposals discuss changes to markets and investment. An objection to those programs has to address the markets they would preserve or change. The energy-certificate design proposes replacing private exchange altogether.

Digital money provides a more exact comparison with the certificates. In a [January 2023 speech](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/key/date/2023/html/ecb.sp230123~2f8271ed76.en.html) about the proposed digital euro, ECB Executive Board member Fabio Panetta explicitly rejected restrictions on where, when or to whom people could pay. That stated design differs from a personal energy allotment that expires after a budget period. It is a proposal to assess, not a guarantee about every possible digital currency. Privacy, limits and legal safeguards remain questions for the particular system examined in [[cbdc]].

Panetta also distinguished the currency from a payment instruction chosen by its user, such as an automatic rent payment. Arranging when a transfer occurs does not require restricting the recipient’s later use of the money. That difference can be checked in a system’s design without accepting its promoter’s wider assurances.

## The decision that remains political

There is a strong practical reason to trust expertise within its field. A resident cannot independently repeat every materials test, audit every economic model or inspect every component of a public system. Representative government also depends on people doing work others cannot personally reproduce. Nor can a public vote make an unsafe design safe. Public authority still needs people who can recognize when a proposed means will fail.

Trust has to survive disagreement and error. In the bus example, residents need to be able to challenge both an incorrect estimate and a correct estimate built around an inadequate objective. The first challenge might require better data. The second might require changing the subsidy, the service obligation or the weight given to access. An institution that answers both by pointing to its technical qualifications has misunderstood the objection.

In Scott's design, the person disputing an allocation would confront the apparatus that also organized production and recorded consumption. In a more limited institution, a challenge might go to a representative body able to revise the purpose or to an independent appeal against its application. The availability and powers of those routes matter more than an institution’s claim to be rational.

A perfectly calculated timetable could still leave people without a journey the community had good reason to provide. They need someone answerable for that choice, as well as someone capable of calculating the timetable.
